Four decisions shape everything below. Revisit them only at the month 6 checkpoint.

1. **Primary stack: TypeScript end to end (Next.js + NestJS + PostgreSQL).** It builds on your strongest skill, NestJS is already familiar, and it is the most common stack in remote startup job posts. One language across the whole product makes you fast.
2. **DSA in TypeScript, at interview level.** Aim for about 100 problems covering the common patterns, not competitive programming. Your strongest language lets you think faster under pressure, and interviewers accept it.
3. **Specialise in AI product engineering.** You won't compete with ML researchers. You will be the engineer who turns an LLM into a reliable product feature: chat UIs, RAG over company data, tool-calling, evaluation and cost control. Your frontend depth is an advantage here.
4. **Proof over certificates.** Every month produces something public: a deployed feature, a write-up, or a pull request. Recruiters and hiring managers check GitHub and live URLs, rarely certificates.

What you will deliberately not do this year: learn Python ML, chase a new frontend framework, start a second big side business, or collect courses without shipping.
